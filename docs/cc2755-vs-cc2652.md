# CC2755P10 vs CC2652RB

Which radio for the next MPM: the CC2652RB of the MPM v2 or the CC2755P10
(TI's newer LPF3 family). Two boards as they are, compared on what an MPM
does: sending protocol packets to the chips the receivers use, and receiving
their telemetry. Then what each chip is like to develop for, and how flexible
it is.

- **MPM v2**: CC2652RB (Cortex-M4F 48 MHz, 352 KB flash, 80 KB SRAM,
  crystal-less BAW oscillator) with a SKY66112 front end (PA, about +20 dBm
  out at the default word `0x2853`, and an external LNA on receive in front
  of the chip's own), u.FL antenna.
- **CC2755P10 LaunchPad** (LP-EM-CC2755P10): Cortex-M33 96 MHz, 1 MB flash,
  162 KB SRAM, HSM, its own +20 dBm PA (`pwr 20`), the chip's LNA only (no front end), PCB antenna,
  48 MHz crystal.

**Bottom line.** Over the air they're on par for the MPM protocols; the one
weak spot found is the CC2652's 2 Mbps receive from one address (below).
The CC2755 has more headroom: 2 Mbps natively, twice the clock, three times
the flash, twice the RAM, a +20 dBm PA in the chip. It costs more work: TI gives it no proprietary PHYs (ours are
reverse-engineered), long packets need the CPU, the tooling is young (our own
OpenOCD flash driver, secure-boot pitfalls), and the MPM firmware is not
ported yet. The CC2652 is proven and ships today.

Both run the same bench firmware (`src/bench.c`, `frame.c`) over their own
`radio.c`; the other end is the 4-in-1's CC2500, A7105, CYRF6936 and
NRF24L01 (the bench firmware of the MPM fork, [bench.md](bench.md)).

## Over the air (short run, 2026-10-30)

The best each board achieves against the 4-in-1's chips: "sending" is the
board to the chip (the protocol packets), "telemetry" the chip to the board.
Each board at its own trim (calibrated against the 4-in-1) and at a level
within the receiving chip's range; frequency offsets and overloading the
chips a few cm away are calibration matters, left out here. A short run: 50
to 300 packets per test and direction, both boards in turns in the same run
(`tools/bench/compare.py`). Not covered: sensitivity and range, timing on
the CC2755.

| Family (tests) | Sending: CC2755 | Sending: MPM v2 | Telemetry: CC2755 | Telemetry: MPM v2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC2500 (18) | 100% (Corona V2 97 to 99%) | 100% (Corona V2 97 to 98%) | 100% | 100% |
| A7105 (11) | 100% on 8; Kyosho 98, FlySky 97 to 99, WFLY2 95 to 98% | 98 to 100%, Joysway 68% | 100% | 100% |
| CYRF6936 (12) | 100% | 100% | 98 to 100% | 92 to 100% (WFLY 92, DSM bind 94) |
| NRF24L01 / XN297 (33) | 96 to 100% (2 Mbps 96 to 100) | 92 to 100% (2 Mbps 92 to 100) | 92 to 100% (XN297 enhanced unscrambled 92) | 100%, but 2 Mbps from `0102030405` 38 to 51% |

- **MPM v2 receiving 2 Mbps from address `0102030405`**: CRC errors in four
  runs, worse with the NRF24L01 at lower power: not a level matter. The
  other five addresses 100%, the CC2755 100% on all six. The CC2652's 2 Mbps
  receive goes through the Bluetooth LE 2M PHY with TI's CC27xx modem
  overrides; the CC2755 receives 2 Mbps natively.
- **A7105 at 500 kbps (FlySky, WFLY2)**: the CC2755 does best with its
  rectangular 2-FSK pulse (`phy ... 1`); its GFSK's isolated bits reach 72
  to 80% of the full deviation (the CC2652's 94%, on the SDR).
- **MPM v2 Joysway** 68% in this run, not searched further.
- **Telemetry**: 92% and up on both boards (apart from that address), with
  no pattern by board. The MPM v2's external LNA would only show near
  sensitivity, which this run doesn't reach.

Verdict for the radio alone: on par. The one weak spot is the CC2652
receiving 2 Mbps from one address, a cost of the BLE 2M PHY route.

## Developer experience

Our own history is the best measure: the CC2652 bench went from nothing to
all four chip families in about a week (2026-10-18 to 10-24, the rig,
`frame.c` and the framings written at the same time). The CC2755 went from
radio bring-up to every suite passing in two days (2026-10-29 and 10-30),
reusing `frame.c`, `bench.c`, the rig and what the CC2652 had taught: the
modem is the same IP, with the same register names.

| | CC2652RB | CC2755P10 |
|---|---|---|
| Radio API | RF core (Cortex-M0, TI ROM and binary patches) behind a documented command interface: `CMD_PROP_TX/RX(_ADV)`, data queues, triggers, the RAT timer | LRF: PBE, MCE, RFE cores running TI's images; TI's LRF driver layer and RCL handlers ship as source; no TRM chapter, every register "Internal, only through TI's API" |
| Registers | modem and RFE registers undocumented: tuning (sync threshold `0x5114`, AGC) by override values from SmartRF, found by measurement ([rf-core-notes.md](rf-core-notes.md)) | every register and field named in the headers (`hw_lrfd*.h`, PBE RAM maps): readable, but behaviour learned from TI's driver code and on the bench |
| PHYs from TI | proprietary 2-(G)FSK at any rate in SmartRF Studio / SysConfig | BLE, 802.15.4, NESB 1 and 2 Mbps only; nothing below 1 Mbps, no free rate or deviation. We ported CC23xx PHYs (`tools/cc27_phy.py`) and wrote `phy_fsk.c`: rate word, resampler, deviation, decimation, the TX pulse's FIR taps |
| Raw mode | raw packets supported by the commands | works, with traps: the PBE needs a CRC to end a packet (else it hangs), sync word B must be set, NESB mode off, trims written 0 |
| Long packets (DSM bind, 1029 bytes) | built in: data queues and partial read entries, no CPU timing | not in TI's handlers: `rf.c` streams the FIFOs itself (2026-10-30), the CPU polling within 2 ms at 1 Mbps; interrupts on FIFO thresholds are possible but not done |
| Variable length RX | the RF core asks for the length from the first bytes (interrupt) | receives the maximum length; the PBE has length-field options, not used yet |
| Flashing, debugging | mainline OpenOCD, J-Link, XDS110; the MPM v2 has its own boot loader, flashing from the radio | no mainline OpenOCD: our `cc27xx` flash driver (fork, not upstream) and an XDS110 wake fix; TI's ti-openocd wiped the HSM firmware once; UniFlash works; J-Link only erases the whole chip. Secure boot, HSM, CCFG with CRCs, SACI: more ways to brick or lock a board |
| SDK | mature (cc13xx_cc26xx), large user base; FrSky's own gear runs on CC26xx | young (LPF3 SDK 9.x), BLE-centred; SysConfig works from the command line |

Summary: the CC2652's radio is easier to drive (documented commands that do
streaming, long packets and variable lengths themselves) but harder to tune
(undocumented modem, patches). The CC2755 is the reverse: everything is
visible but nothing below the driver layer is documented, TI gives no
proprietary PHYs, and the CPU does more of the work. Its tooling is the
weakest point today (flash driver of our own, secure-boot pitfalls).

## Flexibility

| | CC2652RB | CC2755P10 |
|---|---|---|
| Rates | 10 kbps to 1 Mbps proprietary; 2 Mbps RX only through the BLE 2M PHY | 10 kbps to 2 Mbps raw, both ways; large deviations against the rate (Redpine slow, h 7.6) need RX oversampling and a software vote |
| Sync words | one, plus an alternative in the advanced RX command; threshold settable | two (A and B) in every RX; threshold not found yet |
| TX shaping | GFSK or 2-FSK | GFSK BT 0.5 or any 48-tap FIR (2-FSK done with a rectangle) |
| Timing | measured: TX start, TX to RX, RSSI, timestamps meet [radio-interface.md](radio-interface.md); ESB acks chained in the RF core | operations at a SYSTIM time (0.25 us), sync timestamps; turnaround not measured (no test pins on the bench yet) |
| CPU, memory | M4F 48 MHz, 352 KB flash, 80 KB SRAM (the MPM v2 firmware fits, all protocols) | M33 96 MHz, 1 MB flash, 162 KB SRAM, TrustZone, HSM, vector unit (APU) |
| Clock | no crystal (BAW, about +-40 ppm, corrected in software) | 48 MHz crystal needed |
| RF front end | +5 dBm chip: the MPM v2 needs its SKY66112 (PA and LNA) | +20 dBm PA in the chip (143 mA at +20 dBm); LNA in the chip like the CC2652's, no external one |
| Package | VQFN48 7 x 7 mm, 31 GPIOs | QFN40 6 x 6 mm, 31 GPIOs |
| MPM firmware | runs today (releases v0.1 to v0.4, real receivers) | bench only: the MPM core, USB, storage and boot loader still to port |
